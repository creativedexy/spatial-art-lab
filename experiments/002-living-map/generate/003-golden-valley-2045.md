# 003 — the Golden Valley in 2045, three frames

**For:** the local session on the Mac, on the free Codex subscription.
**Cost:** £0. No paid call, no video.
**Inputs:** [`golden-valley-2045/`](golden-valley-2045/) — three plates, each with
its own `*.json` camera sidecar and `*-anchors.json`.

**Do all three in one session**, as you did on 001. They are the same place at
the same hour, and if the sky or the crop colour shifts between them the set is
dead however good each frame looks alone.

## What is surveyed and what is proposed

This matters more here than on 001 or 002, because for the first time the plate
contains something that does not exist.

**Surveyed, and not ours to move:** the ground and its slopes, the brook, the
hedgerows, the rights of way, every existing building in Cheltenham, and GCHQ.

**Proposed, and ours:** the development. It is a rule-based scheme laid on a
*reconstructed* field grid — the bearing is measured (22°, from the surviving
hedges and the allocation's own boundary), the rules that fill the cells are
written down in `scripts/golden_valley_2045.py`, and the buildings are the
output of those rules. 29 fields over 77.4 ha: 29 % orchard, 20.5 % wetland
along the brook, 19.4 % homes, 12.6 % agrivoltaic, 11.7 % campus, 6.8 %
retained arable.

The idea the whole thing hangs on: **the field grain here has already been
ploughed out** — 54 hedgerows cut through the allocation and it stayed one
field over a kilometre across — so the hedges come back first and everything
else sits in the cells they make. Draw it as a town that grew back along its
hedges, not as a business park dropped in a field.

**GCHQ's roof is a wildflower meadow in 2045.** That is deliberate, not an
error in the plate. Do not correct it back to metal.

## The vocabulary

The same one the vision frames already established, which is the masterplan's:
**pale timber and buff stone, three to five storeys, strong horizontal banding,
deeply recessed glazing, glazed active ground floors, wildflower and sedum
roofs, PV on the flat ones, angular buff sett plazas among wildflower planting,
retained mature oaks.** Gloucestershire has real forestry, so cross-laminated
timber and glulam — never curtain wall, never a glass tower.

Late summer, warm low afternoon sun, long shadows in the direction each plate
already shows, blue sky with a few small cumulus, clear air to the horizon. No
haze, no fog, no bloom, no text, no watermark.

**On the panels specifically:** these are *agrivoltaics*, not a solar farm. The
rows are raised canopies with a crop still growing beneath and between them —
about 2 m of panel every 11 m, so most of what you see between the rows is
still field. A dark unbroken slab of glass is the wrong answer and it is the
difference between a landscape that gained an industry and one that lost a
field.

## Inspect, then correct once

The four named failures from 002 still apply, plus two new ones this set can
produce:

1. GCHQ's courtyard filled with water, or enlarged, or its meadow roof
   "corrected" back to metal
2. landscape invented above the horizon — or a horizon invented in Frame B,
   which has none
3. the development turned into a generic glass-and-steel business park, or
   into a suburban housing estate. It is neither: it is timber and stone,
   courtyards, meadow roofs, hedges
4. the panel fields drawn as an unbroken dark slab
5. the brook widened into a river
6. no people anywhere, which fails the rule however good the frame is

## What to write back

Append a `## Result` here: passes per frame, residual drift measured against
the numbers above, and — as on 002 — where the **plate** is the limiting
factor rather than the prompt. That last one is the finding that changes what
gets built here, and 002's answer (the map has no car-park ranks) was worth
more than the frame.
