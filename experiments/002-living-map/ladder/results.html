In plain English

Rung 2 is the result that would have sent us to Blender, and it would have been wrong to follow it. The image is genuinely beautiful and genuinely not Cheltenham, and the obvious reading — “the model ignores our structure, so our structure must be too thin” — is the expensive one. The cheap reading turned out to be true: our structure was never in the file we handed over.

Depth maps are 8-bit. Ours was linear over 1.2 km, so almost the entire range went on the ground receding from the camera and almost none on the things standing up out of it. Control nets are trained on disparity, which is 1/z — it spends range where the camera is close, which is exactly where the buildings are. Inverting our documented metric planes and re-encoding took one point operation and eight seconds of compute.

This is what makes the finding transfer. Hand-modelling this hill in Blender buys one postcode; fixing the depth encoding buys every site the pipeline will ever render. The recommendation flips only if a failure appears that more geometry would fix — and across six rungs, none did.
